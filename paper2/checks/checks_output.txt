==================== closing paragraph, item 1 (POST-HOC, descriptive; no verdict changes) ====================
Does hidden-unit correlation support the claim that the output layer can host the runaway even with independent raw
inputs? In the synthetic independent cells, yes.
- The four never-certified independent P0/P1 students (s0, s4) end with strongly correlated hidden units: polished max
  |off-diagonal correlation| median 0.70 (range 0.43-0.95), effective number of units 1.43 of 3.
- The 12 certified independent P0/P1 students stay almost uncorrelated: 0.053 (range 0.038-0.069), 2.94 of 3.
- So, with independent inputs, the models that run away are exactly the ones whose hidden units became correlated,
  which gives the output layer cancelling directions to grow along.
- Over all synthetic models the expectation holds: runaway 0.935 (PR 1.47) vs certified 0.054 (PR 2.91) at polish. It
  is confounded by input correlation, though:
  - in the correlated cells the hidden units are already correlated at the early state (max 0.80-0.82);
  - polishing raises this further only where the model runs away (P0 0.80 -> 0.96, P1 0.82 -> 0.92);
  - the certified correlated-P2 models keep max 0.88 without running away.
- On real data, correlated hidden units are everywhere:
  - KAN-delta seeds: max 0.82 at 107 epochs, 0.97 capped and 0.98 at the Part A final (PR 1.7-1.9 of 6);
  - the 10 certified SC-KAN-delta fold A minima: max 0.82 at polish (0.96 early), against 0.95 for the never-certified
    baseline at polish (0.88 early). SC's polish lowers the correlation while the baseline's raises it.
Hidden-unit correlation therefore goes with the runaway, and with independent inputs it marks the runaway models
cleanly. It is not sufficient on its own: correlated units in a model whose penalty closes the flat directions (P2, SC)
reach certified, bounded minima.
